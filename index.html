Prologue

Every cup you drink began years earlier, as a seed pressed into dark soil on a mountainside near the equator. It was grown in shade, picked by hand, washed, dried, shipped across an ocean, roasted and ground — all for the three minutes it takes to brew. Today, on National Coffee Day, we follow it home.

Chapter I

The Seed

Coffee begins as a seed — the very same green bean that, roasted, would end up in your grinder. Pressed into loose, shaded soil, it is in no hurry at all.

The nursery

For its first six months to a year the seedling lives in the half-light of a nursery, in a bag of soil not much bigger than a coffee tin. When the rains come it is carried up the hill and planted for good, usually beneath taller trees that keep it cool.

Chapter II

The Tree

A few days of blossom.

Three or four years after planting, the first rains of the season bring the tree into flower. It covers itself in white, jasmine-scented blossoms, and each lasts only a few days before the fruit begins.

Ripening

Each flower becomes a cherry. Over six to nine months it swells and slowly turns from hard green to yellow, orange and finally a deep, glossy red. Only then is the seed inside ready — and a single branch almost never ripens all at once.

Chapter III

The Harvest

Ripe cherries don’t wait. On most specialty farms they are picked by hand, one at a time, and pickers return to the same trees every week or two as the crop comes in.

Chapter IV

The Process

How the fruit comes off the seed shapes the taste in your cup more than almost anything else. There are three classic ways to do it.

01

Washed

The fruit is pulped off, the sticky layer fermented away and the seeds washed clean before drying.

Clean · bright · tea-like

02

Natural

The whole cherry dries in the sun for weeks, fruit and all, before the husk is cracked off.

Fruity · winey · heavy body

03

Honey

The skin comes off, but the sticky, honey-like mucilage is left on to dry around the seed.

Sweet · round · syrupy

Then the beans rest, the papery parchment is hulled away, and they are sorted by size and density and packed into jute sacks for the long voyage to a roaster.

Chapter V

The Roast

Heat turns a hard, grassy seed into something worth drinking. In around twelve minutes, hundreds of new aroma compounds are born.
